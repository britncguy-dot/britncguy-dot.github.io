How Market Breadth Helps Us Read SPY Without Chasing Price

A rising index can look strong while fewer stocks are doing the heavy lifting. Breadth helps us see the difference.

July 13, 2026 Education only No signals
The No-FOMO Trader Market lesson

Look Beneath The Index

SPY can keep climbing even when the market underneath it is getting less healthy. A handful of large companies can carry a market-cap-weighted index higher while more of its individual stocks slip below their own trends.

Market breadth helps us look beneath the surface. For this lesson, we are watching the percentage of S&P 500 stocks trading above their 50-day moving average.

Is the market's price move receiving broad participation, or is a smaller group doing most of the work?

We are not using breadth as a buy signal, sell signal, or prediction machine. We are using it as confirmation and context.

What Price-And-Breadth Divergence Means

A bearish breadth divergence develops when SPY is near a new high, but fewer S&P 500 stocks remain above their 50-day averages.

That is a yellow flag, not an automatic short.

It tells us the rally may be losing participation. We should become more selective, avoid late entries, and watch whether price confirms the weakness through failed breakouts, lost support, or lower highs.

The opposite can happen near a market low. Price may retest or undercut a prior low while breadth stops deteriorating. That can suggest selling pressure is becoming less widespread, but price must still confirm a reversal.

Why A 50-Day Volume Average Is Different

A 50-day average of total trading volume tells us how active the market has been. It does not directly tell us whether participation is healthy.

High volume can occur during buying, selling, panic, news, or routine rebalancing. Breadth is often more useful for this daily-market question because it shows whether strength or weakness is spreading across the index's members.

Our No-FOMO Decision Order

  1. Read price structure first.
  2. Check whether breadth confirms or challenges that structure.
  3. Define the price that proves our idea wrong.
  4. Pass if the entry is late, unclear, or poorly located.

Breadth should improve our patience. It should not give us another reason to force a trade.

A Practical Example

Suppose SPY is pressing near a recent high while breadth has fallen from 78% to 58% over several weeks. The trend has not failed if price still holds its important support, but participation has narrowed enough to deserve attention.

Our response is not to predict a crash. We stop treating every green candle as equally trustworthy and wait for price to hold support, reclaim a level, or build a clean setup with defined risk.

If breadth begins rising again while SPY holds its structure, the warning is easing. If breadth keeps falling and SPY loses support, price is confirming the weakness that breadth warned us about.

No-FOMO Rule

Breadth can warn us that a move is getting thinner, but price must confirm the trade.

Journal Question

Did I use market breadth to become more selective, or did I turn a warning into an excuse to predict what price had not confirmed?
