Why A Pre-Trade Checklist Matters

A trade can feel urgent before it is actually clean. A checklist creates a short pause between the candle moving and the order ticket opening.

The goal is not to predict perfectly. The goal is to make the trade earn attention before it earns risk.

If the trade only makes sense because you are afraid to miss it, pass.

The No-FOMO Pre-Trade Checklist

  1. Market condition: Is this a trend day, range day, chop day, or news-driven day?
  2. Key level: What level decides whether the idea is still valid?
  3. Setup type: Am I waiting for a pullback hold, reclaim, failed breakout, higher low, or lower high?
  4. Entry plan: Where would I enter if the setup proves itself?
  5. Invalidation: What proves the idea wrong?
  6. Stop before entry: Where is the stop before I click anything?
  7. Target area: Where is the next logical resistance, support, or profit-taking zone?
  8. Emotional check: Am I calm enough to pass if this does not stay clean?

Quick Scoring Filter

Before risking money, score the setup from 0 to 10 in each area.

Category Question Score
Level clarity Do I know the level that matters most? 0-10
Setup quality Is price proving the idea, or am I guessing? 0-10
Defined risk Do I know where I am wrong before entry? 0-10
Patience Did I wait for the setup instead of chasing movement? 0-10

A low score is not failure. It is information. Sometimes the cleanest trade is no trade.

When To Stand Down

Consider standing down when the level is unclear, the stop is too wide, the candle already moved too far, or the trade only feels attractive because you are worried about missing it.

That is not hesitation. That is discipline doing its job.
